AI for surgery · Perception

Matjaž Jogan

I am a Senior Research Investigator in the Penn Computer Assisted Surgery and Outcomes (PCASO) Laboratory, Department of Surgery, University of Pennsylvania. I build machine learning methods that understand surgical video with little or no annotation, and I study how humans perceive the world: how the brain combines noisy sensory signals with prior expectations, and how to measure perception without bias.

Research

Two lines of work share one question: how a system, artificial or biological, turns ambiguous signals into a reliable interpretation of the world.

Object discovery in surgical video

Object-centric and self-supervised models that find and track instruments and anatomy in surgical video without dense labels, including slot-based architectures that scale across procedures and federated training across hospitals.

Data quality and weak supervision

How annotation uncertainty, data quality and label cost shape surgical AI: weakly supervised detection and segmentation from video-level labels, and active learning that halves the effort of annotating surgical video.

Perception as inference

Bayesian models of visual speed perception and of how the brain integrates sensory channels; unbiased psychophysical methods for measuring perceptual bias; and the multisensory perception of material properties such as viscosity.
